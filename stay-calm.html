5 Ways to Stay Calm When It Matters

A caravan guide knew that scared travellers make poor choices. Here are five ways to steady yourself.

  1. Notice the feeling and name it

    In the books, readers move their marker between Calm, Shaken and Panicked. Naming a feeling is the first step to steering it.

    Try it: Ask: “Are you Calm, Shaken or Panicked right now?”

  2. Wait a night when you can

    Many big choices can wait until morning. A rested mind sees clues a rushed one misses.

    Try it: Make a family rule: no big decisions in the first 5 minutes of being upset.

  3. Check the facts before you act

    The calm traveller looks closely at the coins, asks the question, tests the scale. Looking before leaping beats panic every time.

    Try it: Practise asking: “What do I know for sure?”

  4. Ask someone who knows the road

    Every caravan had a guide. Asking for advice is a strength, not a weakness. People with experience have seen what happens next.

    Try it: Who is the “guide” you would ask about money? About a worry?

  5. Make amends when you get it wrong

    Everyone is fooled or makes a mistake sometimes. The wise ones say so, fix what they can and learn from it. Great-great-grandmother Kekan did the same in the family ledger.

    Try it: Write a short ledger note: “What went wrong, and what I will do next time.”

A note for grown-ups

These are everyday ideas for building good habits. They are not medical advice. If a child is very distressed, talk to a doctor, teacher or counsellor.
