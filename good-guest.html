6 Ways to Be a Good Guest in a Faraway Place

The Silk Road was a meeting place of many cultures. Here is how travellers made friends instead of enemies.

  1. Learn a greeting first

    A few words in someone’s own language show respect. Sogdian merchants spoke many tongues, and it helped them make friends and trade fairly.

    Try it: Learn how to say hello in one new language.

  2. Listen before you judge

    Customs that look strange often have good reasons. Ask “why do you do it this way?” before deciding what you think.

    Try it: Think of a custom in your home that outsiders might find odd.

  3. Respect what is special to others

    Prayer times, food rules and festivals matter deeply to people. A good guest asks how to behave and then follows the host’s lead.

    Try it: Ask a friend about a tradition at their home.

  4. Share what you have to offer

    A song, a story or a snack can start a friendship. Travellers on the road swapped tales as well as goods.

    Try it: Prepare a short story from your family to share.

  5. Trade fairly, and treat people as equals

    Trade works best when both sides gain and trust each other. Treating a trading partner fairly is good manners and good business.

    Try it: Role-play a swap where both people feel they got a fair deal.

  6. Friends are the best thing you bring home

    In the end, merchants remembered the people they met as much as the profit. Friendships made across cultures lasted for years.

    Try it: Write down one friend who is different from you in some way, and one thing you share.
